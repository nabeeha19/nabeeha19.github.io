A Gaia-Based Framework for Measuring Stellar Mass Functions in Milky Way Star Clusters

How many massive stars does a cluster form compared with low-mass ones, and is that ratio the same everywhere? I'm building a framework that uses Gaia DR3 data to measure stellar mass functions in Milky Way star clusters and test whether they are universal.

The hard part is getting the counts right: correcting for stars the survey misses (observational incompleteness) and for unresolved binary stars that pass as single, brighter stars.

Predicting the impact of coronal mass ejections

Coronal mass ejections are huge eruptions of plasma from the Sun. By the time they reach Earth, the solar wind has reshaped them, which makes their impact hard to predict. I linked what we see near the Sun to what arrives at Earth across three solar cycles.

292CME–ICME pairs
0.69r: CME speed vs transit speed
−0.50r: transit speed vs storm (Dst)

I built a standardised dataset from SOHO, STEREO, CDAW, GCS modelling, NASA OMNIWeb and the Richardson & Cane catalogue. The result: faster CMEs near the Sun arrive faster and tend to drive stronger geomagnetic storms.
